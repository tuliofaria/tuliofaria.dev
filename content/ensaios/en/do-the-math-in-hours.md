Claude Code for around R$100. Is it worth it?

That question came up in a Brazilian developer forum on Reddit. The discussion went the usual way: cost per token, value for money, comparisons with other tools. All fair. But almost nobody asked the thing that decides the answer: how are you going to use it?

## The price alone tells you nothing

Is R$100 expensive or cheap? It depends on what comes back.

Comparing one tool's monthly fee with another's is easy, because the number is right there on the pricing page. The hard part is measuring the other side of the equation, what the tool gives back to you. And that's the side that decides.

So I split it into two profiles.

**If you code every day and the agent gives you back hours of work, it pays for itself.** Here the math isn't in money, it's in hours. Take what one hour of your time is worth and how many hours a month the agent takes off your plate. If that number clears the monthly fee comfortably, the discussion is over.

**If you use it once in a while to clear up a question, it doesn't pay off.** For that kind of use, paying per token or going with another tool can come out cheaper. A subscription makes sense when the use is constant. Occasional use on a subscription is a gym membership in February.

> An expensive tool is the one you pay for and don't use.
>
> — this issue's thesis

## Measure before you decide

The advice I'd give whoever asked on Reddit: test it on a real project before you decide.

Not a hello world, not a tutorial. A project you actually need to ship. Use it for a few weeks and watch: how much did you really use it? What kind of task did it help with? Were there days you didn't even open it?

Comparing prices without measuring your own usage is a guess. After a week or two of real use, you stop guessing and have your own number.

## In short

"Is it worth it?" has no universal answer, and that's fine. It has your answer, and it comes out of two simple calculations: how many hours the tool gives back to you, and how much you actually use it.

If both add up, R$100 is cheap. If they don't, any price is expensive.
